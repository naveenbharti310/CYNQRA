# Revenue management report: when an offer earns money

The Restaurant Revenue Management Specialist's report (r_05). **What:** the rules that decide whether a discount
earns money or only gives it away. **Why:** Bluedip's estimates and recommendations are built on them, and an owner
new to revenue management cannot judge them alone. **How to read it:** **Sourced** names the source; everything else
is the specialist's professional judgement, and the last section says what must be confirmed.

## Findings

1. **Revenue is the wrong measure; margin is the right one.** A discounted cover still costs its food. An offer
   earns money only if the new customers' margin after food cost is more than the discount given to customers who
   would have come anyway.
2. **The hidden cost is the customer who would have come anyway.** In a quiet window some regulars come regardless;
   if they use the offer, their discount is pure loss. This is why a deep discount can raise revenue and lower
   margin at the same time.
3. **Deep discounts teach customers to wait.** A regular who learns that 3 pm is half price stops coming at full
   price. Caps and short windows limit this.
4. **Quiet hours are the only place a discount belongs.** Discounting a busy hour turns away full-price customers.
5. **Sourced:** restaurant revenue management, which manages demand by the hour through price and duration, is
   described in Sheryl E. Kimes's work at Cornell University, for example *Implementing Restaurant Revenue
   Management: A Five-Step Approach* (Cornell Hotel and Restaurant Administration Quarterly, 1999).

## Recommendations

1. **Show margin after food cost for every offer**, next to revenue, before the owner creates it.
2. **Always cap an offer** by number of customers, and keep it inside the quiet window.
3. **Recommend the discount that adds the most margin**, not the most customers, and recommend no offer when every
   discount loses money.
4. **Until an owner enters their food cost, use 35% of the average bill**, and show it on screen as an assumption.
   Ask for the real figure on first use.
5. **Learn each restaurant's response** from its finished offers, and say how many offers an estimate rests on.

## Sources

* Sheryl E. Kimes, *Implementing Restaurant Revenue Management: A Five-Step Approach*, Cornell Hotel and Restaurant
  Administration Quarterly, 1999.
* The 35% default food cost is the specialist's working assumption for a full-service restaurant, not a sourced
  figure; it is replaced by each owner's own.

## Confirm with a professional

* **Each restaurant's food cost**, from its chef or accountant: every margin figure depends on it.
* **How discounts are shown on the bill for tax**, with a chartered accountant (see the compliance register).
* **The starting response assumption**, against the pilot's first offers, with the Data Scientist.
