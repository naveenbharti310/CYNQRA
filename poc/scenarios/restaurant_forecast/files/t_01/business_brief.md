# Covers forecast: business brief

## Outcome

The kitchen sees how many covers to expect each day for the next 14 days, and a prep plan for each day, so we
stop binning food on quiet nights and running short on busy ones (r_01). Success is a forecast that beats last
week's numbers, which is what the kitchen uses today, measured on days it has not seen.

## Priorities

1. Forecast accuracy first: a forecast that is worse than last week's numbers is worse than nothing.
2. Then the prep plan page the head chef and the floor manager check each morning.
3. The prep margin is a business rule, set by me, not guessed by the team (r_06).

## Risks

* One-off nights (a private party, a storm) distort a simple average; the method must not chase them.
* The first weeks run on a sample history until our own covers are entered.
* It runs on the office computer and holds no customer personal data: only a date and a number of covers a day.
